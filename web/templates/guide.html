What this tool does

EASM Agent looks at your organisation the way an outsider does. You give it a domain you own; it finds the internet-facing names, addresses, certificates and services that belong to it, checks them for common weaknesses, and then watches for change. You hear about something only when it is new or has got worse.

By default it uses only public information (certificate logs, passive DNS, public DNS answers, public exposure databases). It sends no traffic to your servers unless an administrator turns on Active checks in Settings.

Get started in five steps

  1. Add your main domain. On the Dashboard, type your registered domain (for example example.com, not www.example.com). Sub-domains are found for you. Adding only a sub-domain limits what can be seen.
  2. Optionally describe what "yours" looks like. Under Ownership checks and options, list the networks you expect your addresses to be on, as ASN numbers (such as AS13335 for Cloudflare, AS16509 for Amazon) or organisation words (such as AMAZON, MICROSOFT). Any address on a different network is raised as a HIGH finding, which is how forgotten servers and hijacked records show up. Leave it blank to skip the check.
  3. Wait for the first scan. It records what exists today and stays quiet; the progress bar shows what it is doing. From then on it re-scans on a schedule (Settings).
  4. Choose how you want to be told. In Settings set up email, and under Alert channels Slack, Teams, a webhook, syslog or Jira. Without one of these, findings appear only here.
  5. Work through the findings. Each one says where the evidence came from, the risk, and the fix. Acknowledge what you accept (with a reason), fix the rest; fixed items close themselves on the next scan. The Report page makes a summary for managers and a detailed one for the security team.

What it is good for

Forgotten and unknown assets

New sub-domains, new addresses and new certificates, including test and shadow-IT systems nobody told security about.

Take-over risks

Names that point at a cloud service you no longer use, which an attacker could claim.

Expiring and unexpected certificates

Certificates about to lapse, and certificates issued for your names by someone you do not recognise.

Email spoofing protection

SPF, DKIM, DMARC, MTA-STS and related records, so criminals cannot easily send mail that looks like yours.

Exposed services

Open admin ports, databases, and software with known vulnerabilities on your addresses.

Brand and data leakage

Look-alike domains, leaked staff credentials and code repositories that mention your company (optional sources).

Mergers and suppliers

Add an acquired company's or a supplier's domain to see its outside view before you connect to it (with permission).

Evidence for audits

Dated reports, an audit trail of who accepted which risk, and exports (CSV, JSON, SARIF, STIX) for other tools.
